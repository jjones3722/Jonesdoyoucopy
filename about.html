About

The short version: I've loved words since I was thirteen. The long version is below.

I grew up in Cincinnati, Ohio, and graduated from St. Xavier High School before heading to Ohio State for a degree in Communications and Journalism. Like most people out of college, I couldn't land the jobs I actually wanted — every listing wanted experience I hadn't been given the chance to get yet.

So I moved to Illinois without a job lined up, sold for a living for a while — and was good at it — but knew sales wasn't the thing I was supposed to be doing. The detour taught me something I use in every project now: how to read a room, find the real objection, and answer it before it's asked. That's copywriting whether you call it that or not.

Words either move people to act or they don't. I first felt that power at thirteen, writing poetry — and I've been chasing it ever since.

Writing isn't a hobby I fit in around a job. It's the thing that's been constant since I was a kid with a beat-up red dictionary, reading it cover to cover until the spine gave out. That kind of obsession with language is exactly what shows up in the work now — in a red-carpet script for the NBA, a product line for Converse, or a single subject line that gets an email opened.

Outside of client work, I'm a certified music obsessive — I once had an iPod carrying more than 40,000 songs — and I can quote every episode of Seinfeld on request. Neither of those pays the bills, but both of them are proof I notice rhythm, timing, and the exact word that lands. Turns out that's useful in this line of work.
